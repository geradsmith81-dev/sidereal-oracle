SIDEREAL ORACLE - WILLIAM LILLY'S READING   (app for Windows)
=============================================================

This is the companion to your Enochian sidereal chart. It uses the same star
chart (the real IAU constellations, at their true widths, with NASA JPL
planet positions) and the same place search. Its background is the Hubble
Space Telescope's picture of the Lagoon Nebula, and its colours (slate blue,
teal, flame orange and peach) were taken from that picture. The
reading comes from William Lilly's "Christian Astrology" (London, 1647),
the classic English textbook of traditional European astrology.

You do not need to know any programming. If Python is already installed for
your first app, skip Step 2.


PART 1 - ONE-TIME SETUP  (about 10 minutes)
-------------------------------------------

Step 1. Unzip this folder.
   - Find the downloaded file "SiderealLillyApp.zip" (usually in Downloads).
   - Right-click it and choose "Extract All...", then click "Extract".
   - Move the extracted folder somewhere easy to find, for example
     C:\SiderealLillyApp (next to your C:\SiderealEnochianApp).
     Do NOT run the app from inside the zip.

Step 2. Install Python (free) - only if you have not already.
   - Go to  https://www.python.org/downloads/
   - Click the large yellow "Download Python 3.x" button and open the installer.
   - IMPORTANT: on the first screen, tick "Add python.exe to PATH".
   - Click "Install Now", then "Close".

Step 3. Start the app for the first time.
   - Open the SiderealLillyApp folder and double-click  "Start app.bat".
   - If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC": click "More info", then
     "Run anyway".
   - A black window says "First-time setup" and downloads the app's
     components (2-5 minutes). Wait until the app's window opens by itself.

Step 4. Make the app icon (once).
   - In the SiderealLillyApp folder, double-click  "Create desktop icon.bat".
     (If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC": "More info" > "Run anyway".)
   - An icon called  "The Sidereal Oracle"  (an orange astrolabe) appears
     on your Desktop and in the Start menu.
   - To pin it: open the Start menu, find "The Sidereal Oracle",
     right-click it and choose "Pin to Start", or "More" > "Pin to taskbar".
   - If you made the icon with an earlier version (called "Sidereal Oracle"
     or "William Lilly's Sidereal Chart"), it is replaced by the new one.
   - If you ever move the SiderealLillyApp folder, double-click
     "Create desktop icon.bat" again (the old icon will no longer work).


PART 2 - USING THE APP  (every time)
------------------------------------

1. Double-click the icon "The Sidereal Oracle" (or "Start app.bat").
   The app opens in its own window, without browser tabs or address bar
   (a Microsoft Edge "app" window; if Edge is missing, your usual browser
   opens instead). Its address is http://localhost:8504 (8504, so it can run
   at the same time as your Enochian app on 8501).
2. A black window starts as well (minimised on the taskbar when you use the
   icon). It runs the app: KEEP IT OPEN while you use the app. When you have
   finished, close the app window AND the black window.
3. In the left sidebar: type the Year (1850-2149), pick the Month and Day,
   set the local clock time (24-hour clock, e.g. 14:30 for half past two in
   the afternoon), search a place name and pick the right match. The place
   starts as your town (see "YOUR PROFILE" below), and the date and time
   start at the present moment there. The "Now" button beside "Date" brings
   the date and time back to the present (in the chosen place's local time).
   The UTC offset is filled in automatically.
4. Two options follow Lilly's own text:
   - "Sun-beams rule": Lilly's table (the default), or the way he scored
     his own worked example.
   - "Orbs": Lilly's own orb sizes (the default), or those he gives
     "according to others".
5. The page shows: the chart wheel with Lilly's houses (Regiomontanus) and
   aspects; the planetary day and hour; the Lord of the Ascendant; a score
   for every planet from Lilly's table of fortitudes and debilities; the
   FORECAST box (Lilly's "Considerations before Judgment"); each planet in
   Lilly's own words; and "Sources and certainty" at the bottom. The
   Forecast can be saved with "Download forecast (.txt)".
   The button "What does this mean? (plain English)" in the Forecast box
   opens a window that explains the Forecast without technical terms. It
   also grades the moment (Best, Good, Fair or Avoid) for the four
   undertakings of the Auspicious dates page: romance, friendship, life
   transition and project, compared with every hour of the same week.
   The first time it opens it takes a few seconds.
6. To stop: close the browser tab, then close the black window.


THE "AUSPICIOUS DATES" PAGE
---------------------------
Click "Auspicious dates" at the top of the left sidebar. This page searches
a period (7 to 92 days) for good moments for:
   - Romance                   (Lilly's 7th house; Venus): beginning a new
                               romance, or the best times to connect with
                               your current partner
   - Friendship                (11th house; Jupiter): finding new
                               connections, or the best times to connect
                               with your current friends
   - a life transition         (4th house, "the end of any thing"; the Moon)
   - a project or enterprise   (10th house, "the Trade any one useth"; Mercury)
Pick the undertaking, the first date, the number of days, the hours of the
day you would consider, and the place. Then press "Find auspicious times"
(about 20 seconds per month). You get:
   - a calendar: one row per day, coloured Best / Good / Fair / Avoid
     through the day;
   - the best windows, with the reasons for each, and the chart wheel for
     any window you choose;
   - a day-by-day list;
   - downloads: the best windows as a calendar file (.ics, opens in
     Outlook, Google Calendar or Apple Calendar), and every moment as a
     spreadsheet (.csv).
How it judges: Lilly wrote no separate book of elections. He said a
student of his book could "frame his owne Figure of Elections". So the page
uses his rules: the Lord of the Ascendant and the lord of the house of the
matter coming together by a good aspect ("perfection", pp.124-126), their
strength (p.115), the Moon, and his "Considerations before Judgment"
(pp.121-123), which mark a moment Avoid (for example the Moon void of
course). The points that add these up are the app's choice; they are in
data\election_rules.csv and can be edited like the Forecast wording.


YOUR PROFILE (name and picture)
-------------------------------
At the top of the left sidebar, click "Add your name and picture". In the
box that opens, type your name, choose a picture (JPG, PNG or WEBP), and
click "Save". The page titles then use your name, for example
"Gerad's auspicious dates", with your picture beside them (the home page
is always called "The Sidereal Oracle"). Click
"Edit profile" later to change or remove them.
- Tick "Add my birth date, time and place" to add your birth details
  (tick "I do not know my birth time" if you don't know it). They are
  used by "Include my birth chart" on the Auspicious dates page and by
  the Friends page.
- Under "Where you live", type your town and pick it from "Matches".
  The Chart and Auspicious dates pages then start with your town as
  the place. (Without it, they start with the main city of your
  computer's time zone, and say so.) Your town is not put in your
  friend card.
- The picture is cropped to a square from its centre and shown in a circle.
- On this computer, your name and picture are saved in the folder
  "profile" inside the app folder. They never leave your computer. To
  reset, delete that folder while the app is closed.
- In the online version, the name and picture are kept only while the
  browser tab is open. Nothing is stored on the server, because every
  visitor shares it.


THE "ASTROSOPHY BIRTH CHART" PAGE
---------------------------------
Click "Astrosophy Birth Chart" in the left sidebar. It is the Astrosophy Birth
Chart app as one page: the real sky among the 13 IAU zodiac constellations
(Ophiuchus included), read through Rudolf Steiner's indications and the
astrosophy of Elisabeth Vreede and Willi Sucher. It opens at your own chart
when your profile has your birth details ("Whose chart?" switches to another
birth). Tabs: Birth chart (with "What does this mean?"), Prenatal epoch, Life
rhythms, Biography chart (your own notes, year by year; kept like your
profile and never put into a friend card), Today's sky, Sources & caveats.
Each reading is tagged with whose idea it is. The chart is geocentric, so the
page has no elevation box. Its files are in the folder "astrosophy".


THE "FRIENDS" PAGE
------------------
Compare your chart with a friend's and find times that are good for both
of you. There are no accounts: you exchange "friend cards" yourselves.
1. Share: click "Download my friend card" - in the sidebar under your
   name (on every page, once your birth details are in your profile), or on
   the Friends page - and send the small .lillycard file (it lands in your
   Downloads folder) to your friend by e-mail or message (or, on the
   Friends page, copy the text code and paste it into a message). The card holds your
   name, birth date, time and place, and your picture if you tick it.
   Send it only to people you want to have these details.
2. Add: when a friend sends you their card, upload the file (or paste
   the code) under "Add a friend", check the preview, and click "Add".
3. Compare: choose the friend. You see how your two birth charts agree
   (Ptolemy's rule, the source Lilly draws on: compare the Sun, Moon,
   Ascendant and Part of Fortune of both), and a calendar of times good
   for BOTH of you: each moment counts only as well as it does for the
   one of you it suits less. A table shows where your readings differ.
   The joint times can be downloaded as a calendar file (.ics).
On this computer your friends are saved in the "profile" folder; online
they are kept only while the browser tab is open.


THE "ABOUT" PAGE
----------------
Explains in plain words how the app works, where the planet positions come
from, how Lilly's book is used, what was checked, and what is the app's own
choice. It can show two pictures from the 1647 book: Lilly's engraved
portrait and the title page. To add them:
1. Open  https://archive.org/details/b30338724  (the Wellcome Collection
   copy of Christian Astrology, 1647, public domain).
2. In the page viewer, turn to the first pages: the portrait of Lilly and
   the title page come before the text.
3. Right-click each page picture, choose "Save image as...", and save it in
   this app's "assets" folder with exactly these names:
      lilly_portrait.jpg      and      lilly_title_page.jpg
4. Start the app again. The About page shows them by itself.


HOW TO READ THE SCORES
----------------------
Lilly adds points for each strength of a planet (in its own sign +5, in the
Ascendant or Midheaven +5, swift +2 ...) and takes away points for each
weakness (retrograde -5, combust -5, peregrine -5 ...). He then subtracts
the smaller total from the larger, "and so judge" (p.181). The number next
to each planet on the wheel is that net score.


HOW THE SIDEREAL SKY IS FITTED TO LILLY
---------------------------------------
Lilly used 12 equal signs. This app uses the real constellations, which are
unequal, and includes Ophiuchus. So:
- a planet in the constellation Leo is read as Lilly's Leo;
- Lilly's degree rules (terms and faces) are stretched to each
  constellation's real width;
- Ophiuchus has no sign in Lilly, so a planet there counts as "peregrine".
These adaptations are labelled "app rule" wherever they are used.


ON A PHONE OR TABLET
--------------------
Once the app is online (see DEPLOY GUIDE.txt inside the GitHub zip), it can be
opened in a phone's web browser. On a phone it switches to a "phone layout" by itself:
 - the date, time and place boxes are in a fold-out panel at the top of the
   page (tap it to open; tap again to close and see the chart);
 - the Auspicious dates and Friends calendars come first as a list of the
   best times for each day, in large text; the coloured grid follows, drawn
   at phone size;
 - the chart wheel shows each planet by its symbol with its score beside it
   (the key is written underneath);
 - tables show their main columns only, in black text on a light parchment
   background;
 - all other text except the headings is white, for easier reading on a small
   screen (headings stay peach).
To start it from an icon on the phone, see "ADD THE APP TO YOUR PHONE'S HOME
SCREEN" in DEPLOY GUIDE.txt (inside the GitHub zip).
The switch "Phone layout" in the sidebar (open the sidebar with the arrow at
the top left) turns this on or off by hand. Computers look as before.
To preview the phone layout on your PC: start the app, press F12 in Chrome or
Edge, then Ctrl+Shift+M, and choose a phone (e.g. "iPhone 12 Pro") at the top.
Press F5 to reload so the app notices the 'phone'.


CHANGING THE WORDING (no programming needed)
--------------------------------------------
The Forecast sentences are in  data\forecast_rules.csv.  Close the app,
open the file in Excel or Notepad, and edit the "text" column (or the "plain"
column, which is the plain-English wording shown in the explanation window;
data\election_rules.csv has the same two columns). Keep anything
in curly brackets, e.g. {lord} or {planet}; the app fills those in. To
switch a rule off, change "enabled" from True to False. Save the file (keep
the CSV format), then start the app again. Lilly's tables are also in the
data folder.


CHECKS THAT WERE RUN
--------------------
- Planet positions agree with NASA JPL Horizons to better than 0.4
  arcseconds (the Moon in the far future: 6 arcseconds), and every
  constellation agrees.
- On Lilly's own example chart (16 July 1634), the app reproduces his
  planetary day and hour, his Part of Fortune, and his strength totals for
  5 of the 7 planets. For the other two, Lilly left out an item that his own
  table prescribes.


TROUBLESHOOTING
---------------
- "Python was not found": repeat Step 2 with "Add python.exe to PATH"
  ticked, restart the computer and try again.
- First-time setup failed: check the internet connection and double-click
  "Start app.bat" again (it starts the setup afresh).
- The browser shows "This site can't be reached": wait 10 seconds and
  refresh the page. Check that the black window is open.
- The black window closes at once: make sure you extracted the zip first
  and are double-clicking "Start app.bat" inside the extracted folder.
- Place not found: tick "Enter coordinates manually".
